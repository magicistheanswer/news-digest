Can the US battery market untangle from China?

MIT Technology Review · Sep 10, 2026
core / ai — energy & industrial tech policy

The US energy-storage market is growing at a record pace and could meaningfully cut grid emissions, but that growth still leans heavily on cheap Chinese battery cells, since US-made batteries - and even imports from South Korea - remain more expensive thanks to China's years-long head start in manufacturing and government support. New 2026 rules require a majority of storage-project material costs to come from outside China and restricted countries to qualify for tax credits, import tariffs on batteries have jumped from 7.5% to 25%, and a late-August national-emergency declaration effectively bars Chinese batteries from grid-scale storage - while new domestic plants from LG Energy Solution, Samsung SDI, Ford and SK On aren't set to ramp up until next year.
